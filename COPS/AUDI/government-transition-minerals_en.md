Critical minerals are vital for economic security and clean energy transitions. We help governments secure supply chains, develop industries and manage risks

Critical minerals pose unique challenges for governments. Whether your country is resource-rich, hosts processing facilities, or needs secure supplies for domestic industries, you face a complex mix of climate vulnerabilities, geopolitical tensions and market volatility. Without integrated strategies, governments risk energy transition delays, lost economic opportunities and strategic disadvantages.

TMP works with governments to develop robust critical minerals strategies that balance multiple priorities. Our deep expertise spans the entire value chain – from upstream extraction to downstream manufacturing – providing nuanced insights tailored to your specific needs. We focus on:

## Resource-rich country development

We help mineral-producing nations maximize long-term value through sustainable extraction policies, processing industry development, and strategic positioning in global supply chains. Our climate risk modeling identifies vulnerabilities in mining regions before they impact production.

## Supply chain security strategy

For manufacturing economies, we provide comprehensive vulnerability assessments of critical mineral dependencies, develop diversification roadmaps and facilitate government-industry partnerships to secure priority access during supply constraints.

## Energy transition planning

Our sector-best insights into supply, demand and pricing are supporting governments like Japan with its battery subsidy strategy and the UK with systemic risk management for mineral access. We integrate minerals-availability projections into national energy and climate plans.

## Strategic stockpile design

We develop customized approaches to national reserves that balance security objectives with fiscal responsibility, incorporating advanced forecasting tools that anticipate supply disruptions before they occur.

TMP wasn’t always focused on critical minerals. Social license, not supply chains, drove some of our earliest work on mining. We quickly discovered that many companies, particularly the majors, are more advanced in understanding the needs of communities than they often get credit for. But even as extreme weather events knocked assets offline, near-term climate change and its repercussions were being routinely overlooked.

For as long as transition minerals supply chains are so concentrated – just one mine in China produces more than half of the world’s rare earth elements, for example – climate change and its repercussions will remain an outsize threat to global efforts to decarbonize. That’s why TMP continues to make transition minerals a core focus.

Our multi-disciplinary team bridges technical, economic and political considerations to create practical strategies that governments can implement effectively.
