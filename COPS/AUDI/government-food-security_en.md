Food security is national security. Plan ahead with TMP

Long understood across the developing world, food security challenges are underestimated by governments in many high-income countries. But as the recent cost of living crisis has shown, the world’s heavily concentrated food supply and rapidly changing climate are a threat to stability everywhere.

If – or when – climate change takes a bite out of global food production, there won’t be a single cause. Instead, a roiling set of climate-aggravated problems – from floods and droughts to conflict and crop disease – could create a state of chronic disruption. TMP works with governments to develop food security strategies that account for these cascading impacts, applying a systems outlook on risk to help you see the whole picture and anticipate perfect storms.

We focus on:

## Risk-oriented modeling

While other climate models emphasize long-term, global trends, TMP’s unique data focuses squarely on the here and now, layering in social, environmental and security risk.

## Scenario exercises

Our scenario exercises help governments and other stakeholders plan for situations across the spectrum of probability and risk.

## Commodity analysis

Our experts don’t just map the risks – they propose concrete solutions. That includes intelligence on climate-resilient crops.

TMP’s interest in food security goes back to our earliest work in farm resilience and social risk. It deepened in 2022 after Russia invaded Ukraine and the mere prospect of shortages sent wheat prices soaring to record highs. Our data tells us extreme weather events linked to near-term climate change are poised to drive similar market volatility. And this time, it won’t be temporary.

Food relief systems and stockpiling are effective at responding to extreme, time-bound crises, but they aren’t equipped to deal with fundamental shifts in availability and affordability. By the same token, boosting domestic production will only get countries so far. The time for a new outlook – underpinned by better information – is now.
