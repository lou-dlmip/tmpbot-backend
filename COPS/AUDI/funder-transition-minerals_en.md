The future depends on transition minerals. Extreme weather and other climate-driven disruptions are already putting mineral supply chains at risk

Every solar panel, wind turbine and electric vehicle depends on a mineral supply chain that’s over-concentrated and overstretched.

In the immediate term, that means energy transitions everywhere are subject to the whims of geopolitics, expressed most recently in the US-China trade dispute that restricted the flow of rare earth elements. It also means extreme weather events caused by near-term climate change, growing in frequency and intensity every year, can knock out a single asset or production area and hamstring global supply even while they devastate local economies.

Stretch the horizon further and the picture gets even more precarious. In the next decade, the clean energy transition will require quantities of critical minerals that current rates of exploration and development are nowhere near achieving. Unless urgent action is taken – action that puts a just transition at its core – the 2030s supply shortage could become a full-blown crisis.

TMP is helping decision-makers respond. Three years ago, we embarked on an initiative to show how critical minerals supply chains are increasingly subject to climate change, funded by the Hewlett Foundation and the Quadrature Climate Foundation.

Since then, our work has expanded to include:

## Community empowerment

We’re co-funding community resilience pilots in Chile, Zambia and Indonesia, working with companies including SQM to make a strong business case for investing in communities and giving local people the agency they need to strike fair and lasting deals over minerals.

## Supporting governments

We’re advising Japan on its US$44-$191bn battery subsidy strategy, supporting multiple departments in the UK on managing systemic supply chain risks, and providing technical assistance to the governments of Indonesia and Zambia to support resilient value addition strategies.

## Unique data and analysis

We’re helping companies including Anglo American improve their climate risk management capabilities and achieve demonstrable material results. Our analyses have been championed by the London Stock Exchange Group, BloombergNEF and the Global Association of Risk Professionals and giving local people the agency they need to reach fair and lasting deals over minerals.

## Cross-sector collaboration

We work with a wide range of stakeholders in a practical, non-ideological way. Our proprietary datasets and reputation as trusted brokers help us connect companies, investors, governments and NGOs to collaborate on shared opportunities.

TMP wasn’t always focused on critical minerals. Social license, not supply chains, drove some of our earliest work on mining. We quickly discovered that many companies, particularly the majors, are more advanced in understanding the needs of communities than they often get credit for. But even as extreme weather events knocked assets offline, near-term climate change and its repercussions were being routinely overlooked.

Today, our experts are working to address a range of core vulnerabilities across the critical mineral supply chain in a growing list of countries. These include:

De-risking early-stage exploration. Early-stage mineral exploration is inherently high-risk, with success rates typically below 1 percent for projects reaching production. We’re working with partner countries and responsible investors to unlock capital by pooling this risk.

Producer-country value addition and community benefit-sharing. Expanding mineral production in the Global South isn’t just a technical or financial challenge – it’s a social and political one, too. We’re supporting producer countries and local communities to move up the value chain and secure tangible, equitable benefits from development.

Recycling and reprocessing finance and operational scale. Recycling and reprocessing are a crucial source of supply that can reduce pressure on extraction and make supply chains more resilient to climate and geopolitical risks. We’re advancing financial and operational measures to scale both approaches.

Combatting minerals crime. Artisanal and small-scale mining (ASM) employs more people than the whole of the formal mining sector. It’s also increasingly at risk of being infiltrated by organized crime. We’re working with organizations including RUSI, the world’s oldest and the UK’s leading defense and security thinktank, to advance the understanding of this growing threat and develop strategies to protect supply chains and workers.
