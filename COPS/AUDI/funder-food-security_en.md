Climate-driven disruptions are putting global food security at risk. The time to act is now

Most of the world’s calories come from just a few crops, grown in a handful of countries. These countries, says TMP’s data, are particularly prone to shifts in extreme and unseasonal weather.

Taken together, our highly concentrated food supply and rapidly changing climate point to one outcome: food insecurity is likely to get significantly worse. And local communities who’ve done the least to cause climate change will bear the brunt of the coming crisis.

If – or when – climate change takes a bite out of global food production, there won’t be a single cause. Instead, a roiling set of climate-aggravated problems – from floods and droughts to conflict and crop disease – could create a state of chronic disruption. Low-income countries will be hit hardest. But if the recent cost of living crisis in high-income countries has taught us anything, it’s that stability will be impacted everywhere.

The global food system needs systemic change. This, in turn, requires systemic analysis. TMP’s unique approach is shedding new light on food security, helping to paint the whole picture and anticipate perfect storms.

Our experts focus on:

## Risk-oriented modeling

While other climate models emphasize long-term, global trends, TMP’s proprietary data focuses squarely on the here and now, layering in social, environmental and security risk.

## Program planning and analysis

Our targeted analysis improves outcomes for food security projects, pairing localized climate data with intelligence on which practices and crops are most likely to thrive in a given geography

## Cross-sector collaboration

We convene a wide range of stakeholders including companies, investors, governments, academia and NGOs to identify shared risks and develop collaborative responses.

## Supporting smallholders

From crop diversification to circular agriculture, the changes we need put smallholder resilience front and center. TMP’s data help make the business case for investment.

TMP’s interest in food security goes back to our earliest work in farm resilience and social risk. It deepened in 2022 after Russia invaded Ukraine and the prospect of shortages sent wheat prices soaring to record highs. Our data tells us extreme weather events linked to near-term climate change are poised to drive similar market volatility. And this time, it won’t be temporary.

Food relief systems are effective at responding to extreme, time-bound crises, but they aren’t equipped to deal with fundamental shifts in availability and affordability. By the same token, proactive solutions are more effective, and less expensive, than reactive ones. The time for a new outlook – underpinned by better data – is now.
