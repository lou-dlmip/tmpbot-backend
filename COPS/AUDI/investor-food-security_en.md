Climate change is threatening global food security. Adapt – and thrive – with TMP’s unique market intelligence

Global food production relies on a handful of breadbaskets, some more threatened by climate change than others. At the same time, shifting climate conditions are locking in higher prices for commodities like coffee and chocolate. Whether you’re financing agtech projects or investing in the commodities space, this simple truth means your investment calculus is shifting. In the coming decade, true market intelligence will center on understanding how, when and where climate shocks will interact with geopolitical and social dynamics to impact a commodity or supply chain. TMP’s experts are here to help you navigate the risks and seize the opportunities.

Our multi-disciplinary approach integrates proprietary climate data with material and political risk assessment to help you invest with confidence, navigating company exposure and assessing project risk.

We focus on:

## Project-level risk assessment

Our analysis evaluates site-specific vulnerabilities that can interrupt operations and increase costs, allowing you to price risk more accurately.

## Supply chain exposure mapping

We quantify how upstream shocks affect downstream portfolio companies, identifying which operations are most vulnerable to disruption.

## Commodity analysis

Our experts don’t just map the risks – they identify solutions. That includes intelligence on adaptation solutions and climate-resilient crops.

## Blended finance solutions

We develop innovative financial solutions for agribusiness in emerging markets, with an emphasis on social viability and high investor returns.

TMP’s interest in food security goes back to our earliest work in quantifying tenure risk. It deepened in 2022 after Russia invaded Ukraine and the prospect of shortages sent wheat prices soaring to record highs. Our data tells us extreme weather events linked to near-term climate change are poised to drive similar market volatility. And this time, it won’t be temporary.

Traditional investment strategies are adept at accounting for one-off, time-bound spikes, but they aren’t equipped to deal with fundamental shifts in availability and affordability driven by slow onset problems like extreme heat and drought. For that, a new outlook – underpinned by better information – will be required.
